The problem with "X" in PM6-DH2X and PM6-D3H4X

PM6 severely underestimated the repulsion between halogen atoms non-covalently bonded to oxygen and nitrogen atoms.  A modification was made to the PM6-D2 method to correct this fault; the new method was named PM6-D2X, see "A halogen-bonding correction for the semiempirical PM6 method" Chem. Phys. Lett. 506 286-289 (2011) J. Rezac and P. Hobza.  A similar modification was made to PM6-D3H4 to give PM6-D3H4X, see Brahmkshatriya, et al., "Quantum Mechanical Scoring: Structural and Energetic Insights into Cyclin-Dependent Kinase 2 Inhibition by Pyrazolo[1,5-a]pyrimidines" Current Computer-Aided Drug Design, 2013, 9, 118-129.

Both PM6-D2X and PM6-D3H4X significantly improve the non-covalent interaction energy between halogenated compounds and oxygen and nitrogen atoms.  The average unsigned error for the X40 set for PM6-DH2 is 1.46 kcal.mol-1, this drops to 1.24 for PM6-DH2X, and for PM6-D3H4 the AUE is 1.68 dropping to 1.28 for PM6-D3H4X. For the same X40 set PM6 on its own has an AUE of 1.73 kcal/mol. 

Unfortunately, the "X" correction causes very severe errors in geometries and heats of formation in compounds that contain halogen atoms covalently bonded to oxygen or nitrogen, and as a result the "X" methods should not be used for such systems. This was alluded to in the PM6-D2X paper: "... the repulsion is very steep at shorter distances; the correction would overestimate the repulsion at distances shorter than approximately 80% of the equilibrium distance." Here "equilibrium distance" refers to the non-covalent interaction distance.  Covalent bond lengths are, of course, much shorter.

Problem

In order to make the "X" methods acceptable for general use, these methods would need to be modified. Until that is done, they should only be used in systems that (a) do not contain covalent bonds of the type just described, and (b) do contain non-covalent bods of the type found in the X40 set.
