Requirements

Prerequisites and Effort involved

The most demanding task that involves PARAM is developing a new general semiempirical method.  In addition to access to computational equipment, method development requires a large body of knowledge that includes: familiarity with Windows, Linux, and Mac operating systems; a good grounding in chemistry; expertise in statistical mathematics; and an understanding of the strengths and weaknesses of sources of reference data.  In addition, creating a new method requires both the ability and the willingness to commit many man-years of effort to the project.

Sub-projects, such as improving individual phenomena such as solvation, electronics, hydrogen bonds, UV-Vis spectra, barrier heights, etc., although still intrinsically difficult, require much less effort than that needed in the development of a new general method. In the interests of avoiding complexity, sub-projects can be regarded as being the same, in principle, as general methods, just not quite as demanding in terms of resources.

Coordination

Because of the need for highly-trained researchers and because of the large amount of effort involved, it is of paramount importance that duplication of projects is avoided.  The prospect of being ready to announce to the world a new wonderful improved method, one that everyone would want access to, and then to learn that another research group had just released an essentially similar program, would be unimaginably horrific.  To avoid this catastrophe, it is essential that research groups are aware of what other groups are doing.  Also, coordination of activities would naturally lead to sharing of knowledge of the issues involved in method development.   To this end, the following procedure is suggested.  Of course, there is no requirement to participate, but in the interests of minimizing unwanted surprises, coordinate and collaboration is encouraged.

Procedure

All contact should be by E-mail to MrMOPAC@OpenMOPAC.net and MrMOPAC@att.net. The OpenMOPAC.net address re-routes messages to the att.net address.

(A) The clearing house for depositing information and requesting information on general method and sub-project work.  Before starting a parameterization project, please provide a description of the proposed work. If a similar project is already running, you'll be informed of the details and contact information.  As usual, the default is that the first project has priority.

(B) Suggestions for improvements and details of bugs should be reported.  These will be given a high priority.

(C) Improving the reference data collections is important.  This can consist of new collections and updates to existing collections.  Updated collections will be provided on request.

(D) New software developments, i.e., improved algorithms and new properties, can be submitted for inclusion in the top copy of MOPAC and PARAM.  Please send a message when a submission is ready.  Inclusion of a new software development is done in two stages, One involves both the donor and acceptor signing a Letter of Understanding as to what is intended, the other involves practical issues such as: what the change does, the software itself, validation tests, protection of the donor's intellectual property rights, recognition of the donors contribution, documentation of the new material, etc.
